Most tools for running many AI agents are built for developers. That makes sense. Developers adopted agents first, and their work fits neatly into sessions, branches and diffs.

My work is mostly the other side. I build agents that move business data: they match invoices to orders, set up new suppliers, sort the inbox, draft refund replies, confirm bookings. When those agents get stuck, the question is rarely about code. It is about money or a record, and the person who should answer is often a finance lead or an operations manager, not an engineer.

Heedroom is the workspace I want for that job. Today it is an interactive prototype with synthetic data: the main workspace and the sign-up and settings screens of a first version. This article explains what it does and why.

## The inbox is the home screen

Open Heedroom and you do not see a wall of agents. You see the inbox: the short list of moments where an agent needs a person.

The example in the prototype is one I have seen in real work. A supplier onboarding agent finds two bank accounts for the same supplier. The signed contract says the account ends in 7001. Yesterday's email from their accountant says 7044.

The agent stops. It does not pick one, because paying the wrong account is money out the door, and the system never chooses between conflicting sources on its own. Instead it sends a question to the inbox.

The question carries its own context: what it blocks, who owns it, and which sources disagree. It also carries a request id, the version of the case it was asked on, the role that may answer it, and when it expires. You do not need to read the agent's whole history to decide.

Below the decision sit two other kinds of items. A refund reply that is drafted and ready to read when you have time. And an agent that stopped reporting twelve minutes ago. They look different on purpose. One needs a choice, one needs a read, one needs an honest warning.

## A decision you can check

Open the question and you see the evidence, not a summary of it. The contract and the email sit side by side, each with the exact document version the agent read and the sentence that matters highlighted.

Every answer needs a reason, and every step is written down: when the agent asked, who it was presented to, who answered, and when the agent picked the answer up. That trail is what you want when someone asks, three months later, why this supplier is paid to that account.

## Agents are grouped by team

The sidebar groups agents the way a business is organised: Finance, Operations, Inbox and admin. A finance lead looks at invoices and refunds. An operations manager looks at suppliers and bookings. Nobody needs to know which repository or branch an agent lives in.

Each agent opens in its own tab, like a document. You can keep the supplier agent and the invoice agent side by side when one depends on the other.

## Every agent shows where it runs

Real setups are mixed. In the prototype, the invoice agent runs on Claude Code with Sonnet, inbox triage uses a small and cheap model, and the refund writer runs in Codex. That is normal, and it matters when something goes wrong.

<Figure src="/writing/heedroom-a-decision-inbox-for-business-agents/agent.jpg" alt="An agent tab in Heedroom. The invoices agent shows its live log with matched invoices and two flagged amount differences, and a side panel with runtime, model, an estimated cost, owner, last signal and a timeline of checkpoints." caption="One agent in its own tab: live log on the left, runtime, model, cost estimate and timeline on the right. Synthetic data." width={1230} height={772} />

So every agent shows its runtime and model, and the status bar shows usage per runtime. It answers the first questions fast: is this a model limit or a data problem, and why did this agent cost more than the others? Costs are always labelled as estimates. Adding exact numbers from one runtime to token-based guesses from another would make a precise-looking total that nobody should trust.

<Figure src="/writing/heedroom-a-decision-inbox-for-business-agents/v1-usage.jpg" alt="The usage page in Heedroom. A table lists every agent with its runtime and model, tokens, estimated cost and a completeness badge such as complete, partial, not reported or awaiting first report." caption="Usage per runtime and model. Missing data is shown as missing, never as zero. Synthetic data." width={1198} height={800} />

When an agent does not report usage, the table says "not reported". It does not quietly count it as zero.

## Silence is a state

An agent that stops reporting has not necessarily finished. It may be waiting, working quietly, or gone. In Heedroom every agent has a last-seen time, and after a while its state becomes "unknown". It does not stay green just because nothing went red.

## Answers belong to a version

The same rule applies to answers and checks. A question is asked about one version of the case. If the case changes before someone answers, for example because the supplier sends one more email, the old answer must not slip through.

<Figure src="/writing/heedroom-a-decision-inbox-for-business-agents/v1-stale.jpg" alt="The decision page after the case changed. A warning says the case moved from version seven to version eight after the question was asked, so the answer was recorded but not applied, and the checks will run again. The audit trail shows the case change and the answer marked not applied." caption="An answer given on an old version is recorded, but never applied. Synthetic data." width={1198} height={800} />

Heedroom keeps the answer for the record, marks it "not applied", and runs the checks again on the new version. The case page shows why: every check is tied to the version it ran on, and checks from an older version turn grey instead of staying green.

## Why not use a coding tool?

For coding, I would. Claude Code now has [Agent View](https://claude.com/blog/agent-view-in-claude-code), and [Orca](https://www.onorca.dev/) runs many coding agents side by side. I compare them in [The agent is waiting for you](/writing/the-agent-is-waiting-for-you).

Business agents need a different first screen. The decisions are about money and records, the people deciding are not always engineers, and the unit of work is a supplier or an invoice, not a branch.

## What comes next

Heedroom is a prototype, and I am testing it against one question: when someone comes back to their desk, do they find the right decision sooner?

The plan for a first real version is deliberately small: one team, three agents, invite-only access, and the sign-up, roles and audit screens you can already click through at [heedroom.com/account](https://heedroom.com/account). The first milestone is one honest loop with a real agent: it finds a conflict, stops, gets an answer from someone allowed to give it, checks that the evidence is still current, and continues exactly once. After that I will measure time spent waiting for decisions, stale answers caught, and how often people get interrupted.
